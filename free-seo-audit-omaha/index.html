What Does An SEO Audit Actually Check?

Four areas. Findability: are your pages indexed, is your Google Business Profile complete, do your business details match everywhere, and who currently holds the results you want. Site health: speed against Core Web Vitals, mobile rendering, crawl access, redirects and broken links.

Conversion: is your phone number tappable, how much friction do your forms carry, and is anything tracked at all. AI readiness: structured data, crawler permissions, and what assistants currently say about you when asked.

Why do most free audits feel useless?

Because they're generated. A tool crawls your site, counts every technical imperfection it can detect, and hands you 340 "errors" — missing alt text on a decorative divider, a meta description four characters too long, an H2 appearing before an H3.

Almost none of it moves revenue for a local business. The report looks thorough and alarming, which is the point. A useful audit does the opposite job: it tells you which four things matter and what to ignore.

What can't software see?

That your contact form asks for a preferred appointment window before anyone has spoken to you. That the business ranking above you has four times your reviews and adds six a month. That your service page never names a single city, so nothing confirms you cover Papillion.

Those are judgement calls made by looking at your market, not your markup. No crawler produces them, which is why the useful half of an audit takes a person a couple of hours.

How often should you audit?

Properly, once a year, plus any time something changes sharply. A traffic drop, a site migration, a redesign, a Google update you saw in the news, or a competitor suddenly appearing above you.

Between those, watch two things: Search Console for indexing problems, and your own phone for whether it rings. Most businesses over-monitor rankings and under-monitor calls, which is precisely backwards.

What if you already pay an agency?

Then this is a second opinion, and you're entitled to one. Take the findings to your current agency and ask about anything on the list they haven't raised. A good agency will already know and explain why it's not a priority yet.

What you're testing isn't whether problems exist — problems always exist. It's whether the people you're paying already know about them.

Section 02 · Site Health
02.1

Core Web Vitals on mobile

Measured against Google's published thresholds — LCP under 2.5s, INP under 200ms, CLS under 0.1 — on a phone, not a desktop connection.

02.2

Mobile rendering

What actually breaks at phone width. Usually tables, forms and anything with a fixed pixel width.

02.3

Crawl access

Whether robots.txt, meta directives or a stray noindex are blocking pages you want found.

02.4

Redirects and broken links

Chains, loops and 404s — with the ones that matter separated from the ones that don't.

Section 03 · Conversion
03.1

Is the phone number tappable?

Real text in a tel: link, present in the header, footer and on mobile — or an image nobody can dial.

03.2

Form friction

Every field you're asking for before a first conversation, and which ones are costing you submissions.

03.3

Clarity in five seconds

Whether a stranger can tell what you do, where you do it, and what to do next — from the top of the page alone.

03.4

Is anything tracked?

Whether you could answer "how many calls came from search last month." Most businesses can't, and everything downstream is guesswork until they can.

Section 04 · AI Readiness
04.1

Structured data

What schema exists, whether it's valid, and — the part that matters — whether it matches what a visitor can actually see.

04.2

AI crawler permissions

Whether GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and the rest can reach you. A surprising number of sites block them by accident.

04.3

What assistants say about you now

We run your buyer's questions across several assistants and send you exactly what came back, including when it's wrong or names a competitor.

Section 05 · The Part That Matters
05.1

Prioritised fix list

Everything above, ordered by impact against effort. What to do first, what to do next, and what to leave alone.

What you can fix yourself for free

Marked clearly, so you can act on it today without hiring anyone.
