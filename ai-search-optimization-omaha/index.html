What Is AI Search Optimization, and How Is It Different From SEO?

SEO earns you a position in a list of links. AI search optimization earns you a mention inside a generated answer. The distinction matters: a link only has to be relevant enough to rank, while a citation requires the system to be confident enough about your business to say your name to a stranger who asked for a recommendation.

That's a higher bar, and it's cleared by clarity and verifiability rather than by keyword targeting.

Why would an AI leave a good business out?

Usually not because it dislikes you. Because it can't establish who you are with enough confidence to risk recommending you. Models are cautious about naming specific businesses, and for good reason — a wrong recommendation is worse than a vague answer.

The things that create that doubt are mundane. Your services only appear inside images. Your phone number is different on three directories. Your site says you serve "the metro area" without naming a city. Nothing outside your own website confirms you exist. Any one of those makes a system hedge, and hedging means naming somebody else.

What actually makes a business quotable?

Specificity that can be checked. "Same-day furnace repair across the Omaha metro, seven days a week, licensed in Nebraska since 2009" is quotable because every clause is a checkable fact. "Your trusted partner for all your heating needs" is not quotable, because there's nothing in it to repeat.

The test we use: could a stranger read one paragraph of your page aloud as an answer to a question, and would it be useful? If not, no model is going to do it either.

Is this replacing search, or adding to it?

Adding, at least for now. Someone comparing furnace quotes still opens tabs. But the shape of the funnel is shifting: more of the early research happens inside a conversation, and by the time someone reaches your website they may already have a shortlist that an assistant gave them.

The businesses on that shortlist have an enormous advantage, and it's invisible in Google Analytics. You don't see the traffic you never got because you weren't mentioned in a conversation you'll never know happened.

How much of this is genuinely new work?

Less than the industry wants you to think. Most of what makes a business quotable — clear identity, consistent facts, real specifics, structured data, a fast readable site — is what good SEO always required. AI just made the penalty for vagueness much steeper.

That's why we don't sell this as a separate premium product. If an agency is charging a large surcharge for "AI optimization," it's worth asking what they're doing that competent SEO wouldn't already cover. Frequently the honest answer is: not much.

Nobody gets filtered out for ranking eleventh. They get filtered out for being unreadable or unverifiable. Both are fixable, and neither requires outranking anyone.

Plain Definitions

What Do AIO, GEO and AEO Actually Mean?

Three labels for largely the same work. AIO is AI optimization, GEO is generative engine optimization, AEO is answer engine optimization. Different agencies picked different acronyms as the field formed, and the checklist underneath them overlaps almost entirely.

Be sceptical of anyone presenting one of these as a proprietary methodology. They're names for publicly known practices.

AIO AI Optimization

The broadest of the three. Everything that makes a business identifiable, verifiable and quotable to any AI system — including assistants that aren't search engines at all. The term we use, because it covers the whole surface rather than one product category.

GEO Generative Engine Optimization

The same discipline aimed specifically at generative answer engines: AI Overviews, Perplexity, ChatGPT search. Popularized by academic work on how source content influences generated answers. Narrower framing, same underlying practices.

AEO Answer Engine Optimization

Predates the current wave — it originally described optimizing for featured snippets and voice assistants. It got reused for AI answers because the goal is identical: be the source that gets read aloud rather than the link that gets scrolled past.

If an agency's pitch leans on which acronym they use rather than what they'll do, that's the tell. Ask instead: what will you change on my site, and how will you know whether it worked?

Buyer Protection

What Are Agencies Claiming About AI Search That Isn't True?

This section costs us work. It's here anyway, because the fastest way to lose money on AI search right now is to believe a confident vendor.

The Claim

"We have proprietary schema markup that gets you into AI Overviews."

What's Actually True

Google's own AI features guidance says no special schema.org markup is required for AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that structured data should match the visible text on the page. Schema helps machines understand a page — that's why we use it. It is not an entry ticket, and there is no proprietary version of a public standard.

The Claim

"We'll get you ranked #1 in ChatGPT."

What's Actually True

There is no ranking in ChatGPT. Answers are generated per conversation and vary between users, sessions and phrasings. There is no position to occupy and no leaderboard to top. What exists is the probability of being named, which you raise by removing reasons to be left out.

The Claim

"Our AI rank tracker shows you're position 3 across all AI engines."

What's Actually True

Those tools run sample prompts and average the outcomes. That's a reasonable thing to do and we do a version of it ourselves. Presenting the output as a precise position is where it turns dishonest. A sampled estimate reported to one decimal place is still an estimate.

The Claim

"llms.txt is essential — you're invisible to AI without it."

What's Actually True

llms.txt is a community proposal for a plain-text summary at your site root. No major AI provider has confirmed using it as a retrieval or ranking input. We publish one because it takes minutes and costs nothing if it matters later. Calling it essential is running ahead of the evidence.

The Claim

"AI killed SEO. You need a completely different strategy now."

What's Actually True

The same site that ranks well is usually the same site that gets cited, because both reward clarity, structure and corroboration. AI raised the cost of vagueness. It didn't invalidate technical SEO, content quality or authority. Treating them as opposing strategies is a sales framing, not a technical one.

The Claim

"Stuff your pages with FAQ schema — it's how you get into AI answers."

What's Actually True

Google deprecated FAQ rich results as of May 7, 2026, with Search Console reporting removed in June 2026 and API support in August 2026. FAQPage is still a valid type and Google still parses it, so we keep it — but tied to real questions with real answers visible on the page. Schema describing content that isn't there is a liability, not a lever.

Sources: Google Search Central FAQ structured data documentation (deprecation notice, May 2026) and Google's AI features guidance for site owners. Ask any agency pitching you AI services to point at the documentation behind their claims. It's a short conversation either way.

Access First

Which AI Crawlers Should Your Omaha Business Allow?

For a local service business, essentially all of them. Blocking AI crawlers removes you from the systems that might otherwise recommend you, which is a strange trade for a business that wants customers. Publishers who sell access to their writing have a real argument for blocking. A plumbing company does not.

Check your robots.txt regardless. A surprising number of sites block these bots accidentally through a plugin or template default and never find out.

Why does this format work?

        
Because it's self-contained. The block answers one question completely, in one place, without requiring the reader to have read the paragraph above it. A model pulling that passage gets everything it needs: who, what, where, when, and how to act.

        
Compare that to a page where the service is in the H1, the city is in the footer, the hours are on a separate contact page and the phone number is an image. Same information, scattered. A retrieval system may reassemble it. It may also just find a competitor who put it in one paragraph.

      


      

        
How many of these should a page have?

        
One per genuine question, and only for questions people actually ask. Six well-made blocks across a service page is plenty. Twenty is the pattern that got FAQ schema abused into deprecation in the first place.

        
The honest way to source questions: write down what your team answers on the phone every week. Those are the real ones. Keyword tools will give you phrasings nobody says out loud.

      

    

  







  

    

      The Work
      
What Does AI Search Optimization Actually Involve?


      

        
Eight things, none of them secret: entity clarity, structured data matched to visible content, crawler access, answer-first architecture, verifiable specifics, corroboration from outside your own site, llms.txt, and measurement by prompt testing rather than rank tracking.

        
Every one of these is something you could do yourself with time. Most businesses don't, which is the whole opportunity.

      

    


    

      

        ✓
        

          
Entity clarity

          
One business name, spelled one way, with one phone number, everywhere it appears. Sounds trivial. It is the single most common reason a model can't decide whether two mentions refer to the same company.

        

      

      

        ✓
        

          
Structured data that matches the page

          
Organization, Service, and areaServed markup describing exactly what a visitor can see. Never more. Schema making claims the page doesn't support is the one structured data mistake that genuinely costs you trust.

        

      

      

        ✓
        

          
Crawler access, verified

          
robots.txt reviewed line by line and the named AI agents explicitly permitted. We check what's actually being served, not what the plugin says it's serving.

        

      

      

        ✓
        

          
Answer-first architecture

          
Question-phrased headings followed immediately by a short, complete, quotable answer — then the detail underneath for people who want it. Good for skimmers, voice search and models alike.

        

      

      

        ✓
        

          
Specifics that can be checked

          
Years in business, licence numbers, response times, service radius, named cities, actual prices where you're willing to publish them. Every checkable fact is a handhold. Adjectives are not.

        

      

      

        ✓
        

          
Corroboration off your own site

          
A business only its own website confirms is a business with one source. Consistent listings, reviews, local mentions and directory entries give a model somewhere else to check before it commits to naming you.

        

      

      

        ✓
        

          
llms.txt at the root

          
A plain-text summary of the business and its key pages. Unproven, cheap, and harmless. We ship it on that basis and we tell you that's the basis.

        

      

      

        ✓
        

          
Prompt-based measurement

          
A fixed set of real buyer questions, run across assistants on a schedule, logging whether you're named, how you're described, and who appears beside you. Coarse, honest, and repeatable.

        

      

    

  








  

    

      The Hard Part
      
How Do You Measure Whether Any of This Is Working?


      

        
By testing prompts, not by tracking ranks. We build a fixed list of the questions your buyers actually ask, run them across assistants on a schedule, and record whether you're named, how you're described, and which competitors show up beside you.

        
It's coarser than keyword rankings and we say so. Anyone reporting a precise AI position number is presenting an estimate as a measurement.

      

    


    

      

        
Why can't AI answers be rank-tracked?

        
Because there's no ranked list to read. A blue-link result has a fixed position on a page that a scraper can record. A generated answer is produced fresh for each conversation and can differ based on phrasing, prior turns, the user's location, the model version, and randomness built into generation.

        
Run the same question twice and you may get two different sets of businesses named. That isn't a bug — it's how generation works. Any tool that reports a single stable position has flattened that variance and stopped telling you about it.

      


      

        
What can you actually track?

        
Four things, usefully. Whether you're mentioned at all across a repeated prompt set. What the assistant says you do, which reveals what it believes about you. Which competitors appear alongside you, which is your real benchmark. And whether the facts it states about you are correct.

        
That last one catches problems nothing else does. We regularly find assistants quoting hours, service areas or phone numbers a business changed two years ago, sourced from a directory nobody remembered existed.

      


      

        
Does referral traffic from AI show up in analytics?

        
Partly, and unreliably. Some assistant traffic arrives with an identifiable referrer, some arrives looking like direct traffic, and some visits never happen at all because the person got their answer without clicking.

        
That last category is the important one and it's genuinely unmeasurable. You cannot count the customer who asked an assistant, got a competitor's name, and called them. The absence leaves no trace in any report you own.

      


      

        
So what does a real report look like?

        
A prompt set with dates, the answers captured, mentions tallied, descriptions logged, and factual errors flagged for correction. Alongside it, the normal SEO reporting — rankings, traffic, calls, conversions — because those still work and still pay the bills.

        
If a report shows AI performance in more precision than the underlying measurement supports, it's decoration. We'd rather hand you something coarse and true.
