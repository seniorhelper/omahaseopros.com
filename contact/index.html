    Before You Call

    What Should You Have Ready?

    Nothing is required. But four things turn a vague first call into a useful one: your website address, the search terms you want to win, roughly what one new customer is worth, and the names of any competitors who keep beating you.

    With those, we can usually say something specific in the first ten minutes instead of asking to book a discovery call.

    01

    Your website address

    Even if you're embarrassed by it. Especially if you're embarrassed by it — that's usually the most useful ten minutes of the conversation.

    02

    The search you want to win

    In the words a customer would type. "Emergency plumber Papillion" beats "improve our online presence" by a mile.

    03

    What one customer is worth

    Roughly, first job only. It's the number that decides whether any of this is worth doing, and most owners have never said it out loud.

    04

    Who keeps beating you

    One or two names. It's the fastest route to a specific answer, because we can go look at exactly what they've built.

    05

    What you've already tried

    Including what didn't work and who you paid. Nobody's judging — it saves us both from suggesting something you've already bought once.

    06

    Your honest timeline

    If you need leads this month, SEO is the wrong tool and we'll say so. That's a five-minute conversation instead of a wasted quarter.
