The Google Business Profile Checklist

22 points, sorted by evidence. Your primary category is the number one ranking factor — and picking the wrong one is the number one negative factor. Most checklists never tell you which items do nothing.

Get the primary category exactly right, fill in every field, collect reviews steadily rather than in bursts, and keep the profile alive. That is roughly 80% of it.

The rest of this is which items have evidence behind them, which only help clicks, and which can get you suspended.

How Much Does The Profile Actually Matter?

Whitespark's 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey asked roughly 47 local SEO practitioners to score 187 factors — 47 of them new for this edition. Google Business Profile signals came out as the largest single group at about 32% of local pack and Maps ranking weight.

Here is how the groups moved since the 2023 edition:

Google Business Profile signals32%—
Review signals20%↑ 16%
On-page signals19%↓
Link signals15%↓
Citation signals7%—

Source: Whitespark 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors, released November 2025 — approximately 47 practitioners scoring 187 factors. Reviews rose from 16% in the 2023 edition; on-page and link signals both fell. Important: Google publishes only three local factors — relevance, distance and prominence — and no per-field percentages. These weightings are an expert estimate, not Google's numbers.

Two things follow from that. Links matter less for the map pack than almost anyone assumes. And the largest chunk of your local visibility sits inside a free product you control completely.

The Category Problem

In the same survey, your primary business category ranked as the single most important individual local pack factor. Ahead of reviews. Ahead of links. Ahead of anything on your website.

And choosing the wrong one was ranked the number one negative factor, with the highest damage score of any signal tested.

One field. Biggest lever available and biggest own goal available, simultaneously.

Pick the narrowest accurate category

Google offers over 4,000 categories. "Emergency Plumber" beats "Plumbing Service" for urgent searches. "Personal Injury Attorney" beats "Lawyer" for injury queries.

Your primary category should describe your core business — not the most profitable service you occasionally sell, and not the broadest label that technically covers you.

Then add secondary categories. You get up to nine, and additional categories rank as the 8th most important local pack factor in the same survey. BrightLocal's category study found businesses using four additional categories had the highest average map ranking at 5.9.

But only categories that genuinely describe what you do. Adding "Roofing Contractor" to a plumbing profile because roofing pays well dilutes your relevance rather than expanding it.

The fastest way to find gaps: look at the three businesses currently holding the map pack for your main term and note which categories they carry that you do not. Our local SEO guide covers how to read that landscape properly.

    Reviews: Recency Beats Total
    

        
    Review signals rose from about 16% of local pack weight in the 2023 survey to roughly 20% in 2026, while link signals fell. That trade is worth noticing — the thing you can get by doing good work went up, and the thing you have to buy or beg for went down.
    

        
    The part most businesses get wrong is treating reviews as a total to accumulate. A business collecting two or three reviews a week outperforms a competitor sitting on 500 reviews with nothing new in three months. Google is trying to work out whether customers are choosing you now, not whether they did in 2021.
    

        
    Meanwhile the consumer bar is rising fast. BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey found:
    

        
      
          
    • 41% of consumers always read reviews before choosing a local business — up from 29% the previous year
      • 
          
    • 31% will only use a business rated 4.5 stars or higher — up from 17%
      • 
          
    • 68% require at least a 4-star rating — up from 55%
      • 
        
    

        
    Those jumps are enormous for a single year. A 4.2-star average that was acceptable last year now falls below the threshold for two thirds of the people reading it.
    

        
    Source: BrightLocal 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey. Whitespark 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors for the weighting shift.
    

        
    What's Been Debunked
    

        
    This is the section most checklists leave out, because it removes items from their own list.
    

        
    
          Posts do not move rankings
          
    Sterling Sky ran a controlled study tracking 441 keywords over nine weeks and found no ranking movement attributable to Google Business Profile Posts.
    
          
    That is the best evidence available on the question, and it contradicts a great deal of advice still being sold. Posts genuinely help click-through and give someone scanning the panel a reason to pick you. Publish them for that. Do not buy "weekly posting" as a ranking service.
    
        
    

        
    The same scepticism applies to geotagging photos before upload, which has been tested repeatedly and never produced a demonstrable ranking effect, and to stuffing keywords into post text.
    

        
    None of this means the profile does not matter — the survey data says the opposite. It means the profile matters through completeness, accuracy, categories and reviews, not through busywork that looks like activity.
    

        
    The Fastest Way To Lose Everything
    

        
    Keyword stuffing in the business name. It violates Google Business Profile policy, it can trigger enforcement and suspension, and competitors can report you — many do.
    

        
    A suspended listing is far worse than a slow-ranking one. Reinstatement takes weeks, sometimes requires documentary evidence, and occasionally does not happen at all.
    

        
    If competitors in your market are doing it and outranking you, the correct response is to report them through the redress process rather than to copy them. It works more often than people expect.
    

        
    Two other suspension risks worth knowing: using a mailbox, virtual office or coworking address you do not actually operate from, and listing a service-area business with a public address it does not staff. Both get removed, and both are harder to unwind than to avoid.
    

        
    
          Before you edit anything
          
    Screenshot your current profile in full — name, categories, hours, services, description, attributes. If an edit triggers a review or a suspension, you will want a record of exactly what it looked like before.
    
          
    Also check the "Updates from Google" and public suggested edits. Users and Google can change your data, and nobody tells you loudly when they do.
    
        
    

        
    Profiles Are Now Feeding AI Answers
    

        
    New for the 2026 edition, Whitespark's survey added an AI Search Visibility category — and three of the top five factors in it are citation and entity based.
    

        
    That is a meaningful shift. Citations were the group everyone declared dead five years ago. They turn out to be the layer that lets a system confirm your business exists, where, and doing what — which is exactly what a language model needs before it will name you.
    

        
    The practical version: your profile is no longer just feeding the map pack. It is one of the most structured, most trusted descriptions of your business anywhere on the web, and assistants read it. An incomplete profile does not just cost you map positions — it leaves an AI without enough to say about you. We cover that side in more depth in appearing in AI Overviews.
    

        
    A Maintenance Rhythm That Works
    

        
    The profiles losing ground are almost always the ones optimised once, two years ago, and never opened since. You do not need to live in it. You need a rhythm.
    

        
      
          
    • Weekly: respond to every new review, add one photo of real work.
      • 
          
    • Monthly: check hours are still right, review any public suggested edits, add a post if you have something real to say.
      • 
          
    • Quarterly: re-check categories against whoever is currently outranking you, refresh the services list, confirm your business details still match your website and directories exactly.
      • 
          
    • Before every holiday: set special hours in advance. Nothing else on this list produces an angry review as reliably as a customer arriving at a closed business.
      • 
        
    

        
    That is maybe twenty minutes a week. Against 32% of your local ranking weight, it is the best-value time in local marketing.
