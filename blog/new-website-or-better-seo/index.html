Do I Need A New Website Or Better SEO?

One question sorts most of it: are people arriving and not contacting you, or not arriving at all? Everything else is detail underneath that.

Are people arriving and not contacting you, or not arriving at all? Traffic without enquiries is a website problem. Enquiries but too few is a visibility problem.

If both are true — and often they are — fix the website first. Sending more traffic to a site that does not convert makes the problem more expensive, not smaller.

The One Question

Almost everyone gets this wrong in the same direction: toward whatever they were already leaning. The owner who has been embarrassed by their site for two years concludes it is the site. The owner who just paid for a redesign concludes it must be marketing.

Open Google Search Console and Google Analytics before you decide anything. You need two numbers:

  • Monthly organic visitors. Search Console, clicks, last 28 days.
  • Enquiries from those visitors. Calls and form fills you can attribute to search.

If you cannot produce the second number, that is your finding right there. Until calls and forms are tracked, every decision about which channel to fund is a guess — and it is the reason so many businesses rebuild a perfectly adequate website.

A rough reference point

Published 2026 benchmarks put the median website conversion rate at roughly 2.35%, with the top quartile above 11.45%. Dedicated landing pages sit higher, at a median of about 4.02%.

So 300 organic visitors a month producing 7 enquiries is around median. The same 300 producing zero is a website problem you can see from across the room.

One caution: under about 100 visitors a month, conversion rate is noise. One enquiry either way swings it wildly. At that volume, judge the site on whether it is technically sound and clear — not on a percentage.

Signs It's The Website

Four checks, all free, all under fifteen minutes. Fail two or more while traffic is arriving, and the site is your bottleneck.

1. It's slow on a phone

Not on your office wifi. On cellular, on a mid-range phone. Run it through Google's PageSpeed Insights and read the mobile score.

2. Something breaks at phone width

Tables that overflow. Forms you cannot tap accurately. Text at eight point. Mobile is roughly 62% of web traffic and converts at about half the desktop rate — a gap that is partly structural and partly self-inflicted.

3. A stranger can't tell what you do in five seconds

What you do, where you do it, what to do next. If the top of your page says "Welcome to our website" or leads with a mission statement, you are spending your most valuable seconds on nothing.

4. The phone number isn't tappable

Real text in a tel: link, in the header, on every page. An image of a phone number on a mobile site is a conversion problem disguised as a design choice.

Then there's the form

Unbounce's 2026 Conversion Benchmark Report found three-field forms convert at 10.1% while nine-field forms drop to 3.6% — with the steepest decline between four and seven fields.

Every field past the minimum is a question you are asking before you have earned the right to ask it. Name, phone, one qualifying detail. Get the rest on the call.

What Slow Actually Costs

Google and SOASTA modelled bounce probability against mobile load time using real-world data. The curve is not linear — it steepens:

1s → 3s+32%
1s → 5s+90%
1s → 6s+106%
1s → 10s+123%

Probability of a mobile visitor bouncing, relative to a one-second load. Source: Google / SOASTA mobile research.

Google's DoubleClick research found 53% of mobile visits are abandoned when a page takes longer than three seconds. That is traffic you already paid for — through ads, through SEO, through whatever got them there — leaving before they see anything.

The other direction is just as striking. Deloitte and Google studied 37 retail, travel, luxury and lead-generation brands and found that a 0.1 second improvement in mobile speed lifted retail conversion by 8.4%, average order value by 9.2%, and travel conversions by 10.1%. One tenth of a second is below conscious perception and it still moved every step of the funnel.

Read those numbers honestly

Most speed-to-conversion figures come from vendor case studies and observational analysis rather than randomised controlled trials. The direction is very well supported across many independent datasets.

The precise percentages are directional guides, not constants you should plug into a business case. Anyone quoting them to two decimal places is overselling what the research supports.

For reference, Google's Core Web Vitals thresholds are LCP under 2.5 seconds, INP under 200 milliseconds, and CLS under 0.1. Published figures suggest fewer than half of mobile sites pass all three, with LCP the most commonly failed. Our web design page covers what actually causes those failures.

Signs It's Visibility

The mirror image. The site converts the people who reach it — there just are not many.

  • You rank for your own business name and nothing else. Search Console shows branded terms and little else, which means you are being found by people who already knew you.
  • You have fewer pages than services. One combined "Services" page ranks for none of them properly.
  • Your Google Business Profile is half-finished. Profile signals carry roughly a third of local pack weight — the checklist covers the fixes.
  • Nothing on your site names a city. Nothing confirms which areas you serve, so nothing matches "near me".
  • Impressions are flat. Not falling — flat. You are not appearing for anything new.

If traffic actively dropped rather than never existing, that is a different diagnosis. Start with why rankings drop, which works through the causes in order.

The Rebuild Trap

Here is how a justified rebuild becomes a disaster.

The new site changes URLs. Nobody maps the old addresses to the new ones. The rankings those pages held do not transfer — they simply stop. Traffic built over three years disappears in a weekend, and recovering it takes months.

It happens constantly, because redirects are invisible work that nobody demos in a proposal.

Three things to demand before any rebuild goes live

A redirect map. Every existing URL, matched to its closest new equivalent. Not "we'll redirect the homepage" — every URL that has ever ranked or been linked to.

A staging check for noindex. A development noindex tag or robots.txt going live is the fastest way to vanish from Google entirely, and it is a two-minute fix once you spot it.

A 404 check in week one. Open Search Console seven days after launch and look for a spike. That is when it is cheap to fix.

The best rebuilds preserve URLs wherever possible. If a page ranks and the address still makes sense, keep it. Changing /services/plumbing/ to /what-we-do/plumbing-services/ gains you nothing and risks everything on that page.

Do You Need A Rebuild, Or A Repair?

Very often the answer is repair, and it is far cheaper.

Fixable on the existing site, usually in days: compressing images, cutting form fields, making the phone tappable, rewriting the top of the page, fixing what breaks on mobile, adding schema, correcting page titles.

Genuinely needs a rebuild: the platform is abandoned or no longer receiving security updates, the structure cannot hold the pages you need, the theme is so heavy that speed cannot be fixed without replacing it, or you do not have access to edit it at all.

That last one is worth checking today. If your previous developer owns the hosting, the domain or the CMS login, you have a business risk that has nothing to do with marketing. Our guide to choosing an agency covers why ownership of your own assets belongs in writing.

When It's Neither

Sometimes the site is fine and the visibility is fine, and the leads still are not turning into customers. Then the problem is downstream, and no marketing budget touches it.

Two tests, both free, both this afternoon:

  1. Submit your own contact form. Time how long a real reply takes. Not an autoresponder — a human.
  2. Call your own number at a busy hour. See what happens. Voicemail during business hours is a lead you paid for and threw away.

We have watched businesses spend five figures on visibility while enquiries sat unanswered until the following afternoon. Fix that first. It costs nothing and it is frequently the entire answer.
